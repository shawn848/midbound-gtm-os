# Person-Based Marketing: What ABM Always Wanted to Be

ABM was a step in the right direction. It said: stop blasting everyone, focus on the accounts that matter.

That was right.

But ABM had a ceiling. It could only see accounts. It could align teams around target logos. It could prioritize industries and company sizes and tech stacks. What it couldn't do was see the actual human beings inside those accounts who were ready to buy.

Person-Based Marketing is what happens when you remove that ceiling.

PBM isn't a new buzzword layered on top of ABM. It's the logical completion of what ABM started. ABM said "target the right accounts." PBM says "target the right people at the right accounts at the right time."

## The resolution problem

Think about it like screen resolution.

Company-level identification is 480p. You can see shapes. You know "someone from Google" visited your site. Good luck figuring out which of 300,000 employees that was. It's blurry. You're squinting at the screen trying to make out details that simply aren't there.

Account-level ABM is 720p. Better. You've narrowed the field. You know which accounts to focus on. You have a list. You have some targeting criteria. But you're still guessing at the individual level. You pick the VP because the title sounds right. Maybe it is. Maybe it isn't.

Person-Based Marketing is 4K. You see the individual. Their name. Their role. Their company. What they looked at on your site. How long they spent. Their validated email. Their LinkedIn profile. Every pixel is sharp.

The jump from ABM to PBM is a resolution upgrade. You're looking at the same picture. You can just finally see what's in it.

## What PBM actually does differently

Let me be concrete because "person-based" can sound abstract if you haven't seen it in action.

**ICP pinpointing at the individual level.**

ABM matches accounts to your ideal customer profile. PBM matches individual visitors to your ICP. The difference matters because the right account with the wrong contact is still a dead end.

When someone visits your website, MidBound identifies them and checks them against your ICP criteria. Title. Department. Company size. Industry. All at the individual level. Your team gets a feed of qualified people, not qualified logos.

So instead of "Stripe matches our ICP, let's find someone to email," you get "Rachel Torres, Director of Revenue Operations at Stripe, spent 4 minutes on your pricing page this morning."

One of those is a research project. The other is a conversation starter.

**Multi-stakeholder detection in real time.**

B2B buying committees have 6 to 10 people. That's not a theory. That's the consistent data point from every major B2B research firm.

PBM lets you see the buying committee forming. Three people from the same account visit your site in the same week? That's not a coincidence. That's an active evaluation. You can see who's involved, what each person looked at, and how far along they are.

One person checking your pricing page is interest. Three people from the same account checking your pricing page, your case studies, and your integrations is a buying committee doing due diligence.

ABM would see "3 visits from Stripe this week." PBM tells you "Rachel checked pricing, Marcus reviewed the API docs, and Linda read the Notion case study." Now your team can engage each stakeholder with the context they need.

**UTM campaign re-engagement with person-level attribution.**

This one is underrated.

You run a LinkedIn ad campaign. You tag it with UTM parameters. You drive traffic to a landing page. Most of those visitors don't convert. In the old world, they're gone. Anonymous. Wasted spend.

With PBM, you know exactly who clicked that campaign and visited your site. Even if they didn't fill out a form. You can see which campaign drove which person. You can measure the actual humans your ad spend reached, not just impression counts and click-through rates.

And you can re-engage them directly. Not through another ad. Through a personal, relevant outreach that references what they were interested in.

Your CMO asks "what did our $40K LinkedIn campaign actually produce?" Instead of "247 clicks and 8 form fills," you can say "we identified 89 ICP-matched visitors including 12 at target accounts, and our SDR team has already started conversations with 6 of them."

That's a different kind of reporting. That's the kind that keeps marketing budgets from getting cut.

## The philosophical shift

ABM was built on a sound principle: focus beats spray. But it was also built on an assumption that we had to work from the outside in. Start with the account. Work your way to the person. Hope you find the right one.

PBM flips the direction. Start with the person. The one who's actually on your site. The one showing real intent. Then work outward to understand the account context.

It's not outside-in. It's inside-out.

You don't start with a list of target accounts and try to find people. You start with people who are already showing interest and understand which accounts they represent.

The difference is massive. Outside-in is proactive but speculative. Inside-out is reactive to real signals but highly precise.

Don't get me wrong though. You still need a target account list. You still need ICP criteria. PBM doesn't replace your strategy. It gives your strategy eyes.

## These aren't wish-list features

I want to be direct about something.

Everything I've described above exists right now. Today. This isn't a roadmap or a future state or a "coming soon" slide.

MidBound does person-level identification. It does ICP matching on every visitor. It detects multiple stakeholders from the same account. It ties UTM campaigns to individual visitors. It delivers all of this in real time through Slack, HubSpot, and webhooks.

We built this because we saw ABM hitting its ceiling and we knew what the next layer needed to be. Not better account data. Better people data.

## How to start thinking in PBM terms

If your team is running ABM today, the transition to PBM doesn't require throwing anything away.

Keep your target accounts. Keep your ICP framework. Keep your sequences and playbooks.

Then add the person layer.

→ See which individuals from target accounts are visiting your site
→ Identify non-obvious stakeholders (analysts, ops managers, technical leads) who are part of the buying process
→ Match every visitor against your ICP criteria at the individual level
→ Attribute UTM campaigns to specific people, not just click metrics
→ Engage visitors in real time instead of waiting for form fills

That's PBM. It's not a new framework to learn. It's a sharper lens on the framework you already have.

## The category is forming

I believe "Person-Based Marketing" will be a standard term within two years. Not because of any single vendor. Because the technology has caught up to the idea.

ABM was limited by what was technically possible when it was defined. You couldn't identify individual website visitors at scale back then. So ABM worked at the account level because that was the best resolution available.

That limitation no longer exists.

And when the technology ceiling lifts, the strategy evolves. Always.

ABM aligned sales and marketing around the same accounts. PBM aligns them around the same people. The people who are actually on your site, actually evaluating, actually making decisions.

That's what ABM always wanted to be. Now it can be.
