# From Anonymous Visitor to Closed Deal: The Complete Workflow

Your website had visitors yesterday.

Some of them were qualified buyers. Some of them were on your pricing page. Some of them came back for a second look.

Your team emailed zero of them.

Not because they were lazy. Because they had no idea those people existed.

This is the workflow that fixes that. Step by step. From the moment someone lands on your site to the moment your rep starts a real conversation.

## Step 1: Identify the visitor

A lightweight script on your website detects visitor sessions. Pages viewed, time spent, referral source, UTM parameters.

MidBound's AI matching engine processes those signals in real time and resolves the visitor to a LinkedIn profile. Not a company name. A person.

What you get for each identified visitor:

- Full name
- Job title
- Company
- Validated email address
- LinkedIn profile URL
- Pages they visited
- Time spent per page
- ICP score against your criteria

This happens in minutes. Not batch processing overnight. Not a CSV you download on Monday morning. Real time.

## Step 2: Score the visitor

Not every visitor matters equally.

An intern browsing your blog is not the same as a Director of Demand Gen spending 4 minutes on your pricing page. Treating them the same is a waste of your team's time.

MidBound scores every identified visitor against your Ideal Customer Profile. Title, seniority, company size, industry, geography. The score surfaces automatically. Your sales team doesn't manually filter a spreadsheet. They see the visitors who matter first.

Here's how the scoring breaks down:

**ICP 8-10:** Perfect fit. Right title, right company, right profile.
**ICP 6-7:** Close fit. Maybe the title is off but the company is ideal. Maybe the company is smaller than your sweet spot but the seniority is right.
**ICP 5 or below:** Nurture only. Not worth a direct outreach right now.

The score alone isn't enough. You need to combine it with what the visitor actually did on your site.

## Step 3: Read the signals

Behavioral signals tell you where the visitor is in their buying process.

A visit to your blog post about B2B marketing strategy? Awareness. They're learning. They're not ready for a pitch.

A visit to your pricing page that lasted 3 minutes? Evaluation. They're comparing you to whatever else they've been looking at. They want to know the cost. That's a buying signal.

Here's the signal hierarchy:

**Tier 1. Act immediately:**
- Pricing page visit (2+ minutes)
- Pricing page + integrations page in the same session
- Return visitor to pricing page
- Multiple people from the same company in the same week

**Tier 2. Prioritize for outreach:**
- Case studies or testimonials page
- Blog post + pricing page in the same session
- 3+ pages and 5+ minutes in a single session
- UTM-tagged visit from a targeted campaign

**Tier 3. Nurture:**
- Single blog post visit
- Homepage only (under 2 minutes)
- Career page visit (they want a job, not your product)

When you stack ICP score with signal tier, the priority matrix writes itself:

- **ICP 8-10 + Tier 1 signal:** Drop everything. This person matters and they're actively evaluating. Outreach within the hour.
- **ICP 8-10 + Tier 2 signal:** Same-day outreach. Strong profile, meaningful engagement.
- **ICP 6-7 + Tier 1 signal:** Same-day outreach. The behavior is strong enough to override a slightly lower ICP fit.
- **ICP 6-7 + Tier 2 signal:** Outreach queue. 24-48 hours. Solid but not urgent.
- **ICP 5 or below:** Nurture regardless of signals. The profile doesn't justify direct outreach right now.

That matrix takes your team from "400 identified visitors, where do I start?" to "12 high-priority visitors, here's the order."

## Step 4: Route the alert

MidBound pushes identified visitors to Slack, HubSpot, or webhooks. All in real time.

**Slack:** A dedicated channel. Something like #high-intent-visitors. When a visitor hits the priority matrix (ICP 8+ and Tier 1 signal), the alert fires. Your rep sees: name, title, company, validated email, LinkedIn profile, the page they visited, how long they stayed, and their ICP score. All in one message.

The rep doesn't open a dashboard. They don't check a report. The visitor comes to them, in the app they already have open.

**HubSpot:** MidBound creates or updates a contact automatically. Custom properties for visit count, last page viewed, ICP score, identification source. Now HubSpot workflows can trigger based on visitor behavior. "If ICP score > 8 and pricing page visited, create a task for the assigned rep."

**Webhooks:** Full JSON payload to any endpoint. Clay, Zapier, Make, n8n, custom backend. Whatever your ops team needs.

The point is that the data goes where your team already works. No new tool to learn. No tab to remember to check.

## Step 5: Reach out with context

This is where most teams waste the advantage.

They get the visitor data and send a generic "Hey, I noticed you visited our website." That's barely better than cold outreach.

The context is the advantage. Use it.

Your Slack alert says: "Rachel Torres, Director of Revenue Operations at Stripe, spent 4 minutes on your pricing page and viewed the HubSpot integration docs."

Your outreach: "Rachel, I noticed you were looking at how we connect with HubSpot. We actually auto-create contacts and trigger workflows based on visitor behavior. Want me to show you what that looks like for RevOps teams specifically?"

That's not a cold email. That's not even a warm email. That's a conversation with context.

Compare that to the alternative. Without identification, you have no idea Rachel was on your site. Two weeks later, your SDR cold-emails the CRO at Stripe because the title matched an ABM list. The CRO has no idea who you are. That email gets deleted.

The visitor who was actually interested? Rachel. She never hears from you.

## Step 6: Watch for buying committee signals

One person visiting your pricing page is interest.

Three people from the same company visiting your pricing page, your case studies, and your integrations docs in the same week? That's a buying committee.

MidBound flags this automatically. You don't have to manually cross-reference visitors by company. When it detects multiple people from the same account within a defined window, it surfaces the pattern.

"3 people from Acme Corp visited this week: VP of Marketing (pricing page), Director of Ops (case studies), Senior Analyst (integrations docs)."

According to Gartner research, B2B purchases typically involve 6 to 10 decision makers. When you see 3 of them on your site in the same week, your SDR doesn't send one email to one person. They multi-thread. Different message for each stakeholder. Relevant to what each one was looking at.

The VP gets the ROI pitch. The Director of Ops gets the workflow overview. The Analyst gets the technical deep-dive.

That's how you close accounts. Not one email. Multiple conversations, each grounded in what that person actually cared about.

## Step 7: Close the loop

The deal doesn't end with the first outreach.

Visitors come back. They browse more pages. They bring colleagues. Every return visit updates their profile in your CRM. Every new page they visit adds context.

When a visitor you've already reached out to comes back and spends 5 minutes on your case studies, your CRM updates. That's a buying signal your rep can act on. "Hey Rachel, saw you were reading about how [similar company] uses us. Want to set up a quick call to talk through your specific workflow?"

When a stalled deal's contact visits your pricing page again, MidBound alerts sales. The deal isn't dead. The contact is re-evaluating. Your rep re-engages at the exact right moment.

The loop never closes because identification is continuous. Every visit adds data. Every data point sharpens the context. Every outreach gets more relevant.
