# How We Built MidBound from Zero

This is how we started MidBound.

true story.

We raised $70K. Not from VCs. Not from angels with Twitter followings. From friends and family. People who trusted us more than our pitch deck justified.

That money had names on it. People I grew up with. People Eli grew up with. People who handed over savings because they believed in two guys with a half-formed idea and a lot of conviction.

That changes how you spend money. Every dollar felt personal. Because it was.

## The first product nobody wanted

We built an auto-prospecting tool. The idea was simple. Automate the grunt work of finding and reaching out to potential customers. Let AI do the research, write the emails, build the lists.

We thought we were early.

We weren't.

Within months, every second startup on Product Hunt was launching some version of the same thing. AI SDRs everywhere. The space got commoditized overnight. Not over a year. Not over six months. Overnight.

We looked at each other and knew. This wasn't going to be the thing.

Killing that product was one of the hardest calls we've made. You pour months into something. You have users. You have a roadmap. But the market told us the truth and we listened.

Most founders I know hold on too long. They optimize a dead product for six more months because they can't stomach the pivot. I get it. But the $70K from friends and family didn't come with that luxury.

## 10 calls a day

After we killed the first product, I started cold calling.

10 founders a day. Every day.

Not warm intros. Not "hey we met at that conference." Cold. LinkedIn. Email. Phone when I could find a number.

I wanted to understand what B2B teams were actually struggling with. Not what they said in surveys. What kept them up at night. What they'd pay to fix tomorrow.

The answer kept coming back to the same problem.

"We spend all this money driving traffic and we have no idea who's actually visiting."

Over and over. Different companies. Different verticals. Same pain.

Marketers spending $50K a month on ads with zero clue who visited. Sales teams running sequences on the wrong people. Revenue leaders staring at dashboards full of anonymous traffic.

That's when it clicked.

## Venezuela to Israel to this

I should back up.

I'm Venezuelan. Grew up there. Moved to Israel. Served in a combat unit.

Comfort didn't make me. Pressure did.

That's not a LinkedIn flex. It's just context. When you've been through actual pressure, the startup kind feels different. Not easier. Just a different kind of hard. The kind where nobody's shooting at you but the stakes still feel like everything.

I say that because the period between killing product one and finding MidBound was the lowest point. No clear direction. Money running out. Friends and family money running out.

But I'd been in worse spots. So we kept calling.

## MeetVisitors

We built the next version fast. Called it MeetVisitors. Person-level website visitor identification. AI matching against LinkedIn profiles. Real names. Real titles. Real contact info.

The first time we showed it to a prospect, they went quiet for about ten seconds. Then they said "wait, this is the actual person? Not the company?"

Yes. The actual person.

We knew we had something.

MeetVisitors became MidBound. We rebranded because the product had grown past the original name. We shipped V2. Then V3.

## Six figures in five months

Here's the part that still doesn't feel real.

We hit six figures in revenue within five months of launching MidBound. With $0 in customer acquisition cost. Zero paid ads driving our own signups. Three people on the team.

Three people.

I'm not saying that to brag. I'm saying it because the startup world has this obsession with headcount and raise size and burn rate. As if spending more money faster is a sign of progress.

We did the opposite. We stayed small. We stayed scrappy. Every customer came from outbound we did ourselves, content that resonated, or word of mouth from customers who actually liked the product.

Fusion VC backed us in batch 16. That validation helped. But the revenue was already there.

## The lessons that actually stuck

I could write a list of 50 things I learned. But most of it would be noise. Here's what actually changed how I operate:

**Kill fast.** If the market is telling you something, listen. The auto-prospecting tool had users. It had potential. But "potential" doesn't beat a commoditized market. We killed it and moved on. That speed saved us.

**Cold calls beat warm intros for learning.** Warm intros are filtered. People are polite. They tell you what you want to hear. Cold calls are raw. People tell you the truth because they don't owe you anything.

**Your F&F investors are your first accountability partners.** VC money feels abstract. Your friend's $5K feels like a promise. That pressure is a gift.

**Revenue is the only metric that matters early on.** Not users. Not signups. Not "engagement." Are people paying you money? If yes, keep going. If no, figure out why.

**Stay small as long as you can.** Three people built a six-figure product in five months. Adding people adds complexity. Complexity slows you down. Stay small until it hurts.

## Where we are now

MidBound identifies the actual humans visiting your website. Not companies. People. With their LinkedIn profiles, validated emails, and the pages they viewed.

We integrate with Slack, HubSpot, and custom webhooks. We match visitors against your ICP automatically. We surface the people your sales team should actually be talking to.

That's it. That's what all of this built.

$70K from friends and family. Two dead products. Hundreds of cold calls. One product that works.

I'd do every part of it again.
