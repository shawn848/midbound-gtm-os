Account-Based Marketing (ABM) is a B2B marketing strategy that concentrates sales and marketing resources on a defined set of target accounts, treating each account as a market of one with personalized campaigns, content, and outreach tailored to that specific company.

## How It Works

Traditional demand generation casts a wide net -- create content, drive traffic, capture leads, qualify them. ABM inverts this: start with a list of target accounts and focus all effort on engaging those specific companies.

The ABM process typically follows these steps:

1. **Account selection.** Identify target accounts based on ICP fit, revenue potential, and strategic value. Lists typically range from 50 to 500 accounts.
2. **Account research.** Map the buying committee at each target account -- the individuals involved in the purchasing decision.
3. **Personalized campaigns.** Create content, ads, and outreach tailored to each account's specific challenges, industry, and buying stage.
4. **Multi-channel engagement.** Reach target accounts through advertising, email, direct mail, events, and social media -- coordinated across sales and marketing.
5. **Measurement.** Track account engagement (not just lead volume) -- are target accounts visiting the site, engaging with content, responding to outreach?

ABM works best for companies with high ACV deals, long sales cycles, and buying decisions that involve multiple stakeholders.

## Why It Matters for B2B Teams

ABM aligns sales and marketing around the same target accounts, reducing wasted effort on leads that will never close. Instead of generating 1,000 MQLs and hoping 10 convert, ABM focuses on the 100 accounts most likely to buy and works them systematically.

The challenge with ABM is visibility: knowing which target accounts are engaging and which individuals at those accounts are involved. Company-level tools help with the first part. Person-level identification solves the second.
