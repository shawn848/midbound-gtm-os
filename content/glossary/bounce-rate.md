Bounce rate is the percentage of website sessions where a visitor lands on a page and leaves without interacting further or navigating to a second page. In Google Analytics 4, the equivalent metric is the inverse of "engagement rate," where an engaged session lasts longer than 10 seconds, includes a conversion event, or includes two or more pageviews.

## Why It Matters

B2B websites typically see bounce rates between 40-60%, depending on the page type and traffic source:

- **Blog posts**: 65-80% bounce rate. Visitors often find their answer and leave. This is normal for informational content.
- **Landing pages**: 40-60% bounce rate. Well-targeted campaigns with strong message match perform at the lower end.
- **Product pages**: 30-50% bounce rate. Visitors who reach product pages are usually further in their research and more likely to explore.
- **Pricing pages**: 25-45% bounce rate. High intent means more engagement.

A high bounce rate is not always a problem. If someone reads your glossary definition of "person-level identification" and gets a clear answer, that single-page visit served its purpose. The issue is that traditional analytics cannot distinguish between a bounced visit from someone irrelevant and a bounced visit from a VP at a target account who read your entire page and plans to return tomorrow.

Context matters more than the raw number. A blog post with a 70% bounce rate that attracts ICP-fit readers is more valuable than a landing page with a 30% bounce rate that converts only off-target visitors. Without knowing who is bouncing, you cannot make that distinction.
