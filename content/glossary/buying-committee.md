A buying committee is the group of individuals within a company who collectively participate in, influence, or make a B2B purchasing decision -- typically including decision makers, influencers, technical evaluators, end users, and budget holders.

## How It Works

B2B purchases are rarely made by a single person. Research consistently shows that the average B2B deal involves 6-10 stakeholders, each with a different role in the process:

**Champion.** The internal advocate who drives the evaluation forward. They believe in the solution and push the organization to consider it.

**Decision maker.** The person with final authority to approve the purchase and budget. Often a VP or C-level executive.

**Technical evaluator.** The person who assesses whether the product meets technical requirements, integrates with existing systems, and is feasible to implement.

**End user.** The people who will actually use the product day-to-day. Their buy-in is critical for adoption.

**Procurement / Legal.** The team that handles contracts, security reviews, and compliance checks.

**Budget holder.** The person who controls the budget the purchase will come from. May or may not be the same as the decision maker.

Understanding the buying committee matters because different stakeholders have different concerns. The champion cares about solving a problem. The CFO cares about ROI. The IT team cares about security. A sale stalls when any one of these stakeholders has unaddressed concerns.

## Why It Matters for B2B Teams

Deals die in committee. A sales rep who only engages the champion and ignores the technical evaluator will lose the deal when the technical evaluation fails. Mapping the full buying committee -- and engaging each stakeholder with relevant messaging -- is essential for complex B2B deals.

The challenge is visibility: how do you know who is on the buying committee and who is actively engaged? Traditional CRM data captures the contacts a rep has spoken to. It misses the stakeholders who are researching quietly.
