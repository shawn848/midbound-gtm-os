Content freshness refers to how recently a page was published or substantially updated. Search engines use freshness as a ranking signal, particularly for queries where recency matters. AI answer engines weight freshness even more heavily, with research suggesting a 90-day citation window for AI Overviews and similar features.

## Why It Matters

Google has used freshness as a ranking signal since the 2011 "Freshness Update," but AI search has made it significantly more important. Here is why:

- **AI Overviews favor recent sources.** Analysis of AI Overview citations shows a strong bias toward content published or updated within the last 90 days. Older content, even if accurate, gets cited less frequently.
- **Outdated content loses authority.** A comparison page from 2024 listing features that have since changed damages your credibility with both readers and AI models.
- **Freshness signals are explicit.** The `<lastmod>` tag in your sitemap, the published date on your page, and the content itself all send freshness signals. AI models check these.

For B2B companies, content freshness requires an operational commitment. The most effective approach is a quarterly content audit cycle:

1. **Review** your top-performing pages every 90 days.
2. **Update** statistics, product details, competitive comparisons, and screenshots.
3. **Republish** with an updated date. This is not about changing a comma. Substantial updates to sections, new data points, and refreshed examples signal genuine freshness.
4. **Resubmit** updated pages through Google Search Console to accelerate re-crawling.

Content that goes stale does not just stop ranking. It actively loses positions to competitors who maintain their pages. In fast-moving B2B categories like martech and sales tech, product capabilities change quarterly. Your content needs to keep pace.
