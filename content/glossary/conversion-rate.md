Conversion rate is the percentage of website visitors who complete a specific action, such as filling out a form, requesting a demo, signing up for a trial, or making a purchase. It is calculated by dividing the number of conversions by the total number of visitors, then multiplying by 100.

## Why It Matters

In B2B SaaS, average website conversion rates range from 2-5% depending on the industry, traffic source, and offer. This means that for every 100 visitors to your site, 95-98 leave without taking any measurable action.

Several factors influence B2B conversion rates:

- **Traffic quality**: Visitors from branded search and direct referrals convert at higher rates than cold paid traffic. Organic and AI-referred visitors typically fall somewhere in between.
- **Page relevance**: Conversion rates improve when the landing page matches the visitor's intent. A buyer searching for "visitor identification pricing" who lands on a pricing page converts better than one who lands on a blog post.
- **Friction**: Every additional form field, page load second, or required step reduces conversion rate. B2B companies balance data collection needs against form completion rates.
- **Offer value**: Free trials with no credit card required (like MidBound's 14-day trial) typically convert at higher rates than demo request forms because the perceived risk is lower.

The 97% non-converter problem is the central challenge of B2B websites. These visitors are not uninterested. They are researching, comparing, and evaluating. Many of them are qualified buyers in active sales cycles. They simply are not ready to raise their hand yet, or they found what they needed without needing to fill out a form. Traditional analytics treat these visitors as lost. Visitor identification treats them as identifiable prospects.
