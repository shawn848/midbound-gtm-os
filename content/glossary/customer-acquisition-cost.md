Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) is the total cost of acquiring a new customer, calculated by dividing all sales and marketing expenses by the number of new customers acquired in a given period -- serving as the primary measure of growth efficiency and unit economics.

## How It Works

The basic formula:

**CAC = Total Sales and Marketing Spend / Number of New Customers**

If you spent 100K on sales and marketing in a quarter and acquired 20 customers, your CAC is 5,000 dollars. The metric becomes meaningful in relation to customer lifetime value (LTV). A healthy SaaS business targets an LTV:CAC ratio of at least 3:1.

## Why It Matters for B2B Teams

CAC determines whether a growth strategy is sustainable. High CAC often signals inefficient targeting -- spending to reach people who were never going to buy. The fastest way to reduce CAC is to improve targeting precision: reaching the right people at the right time.
