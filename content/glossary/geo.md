Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) is the practice of optimizing web content to increase its visibility and citation frequency within generative AI search engines -- systems like Google's AI Overviews, Perplexity, and ChatGPT's browsing mode that generate synthesized answers rather than returning a list of links.

## How It Works

GEO builds on research from Carnegie Mellon and other institutions studying how generative search models select sources. The key findings:

1. **Cite statistics and data.** Content that includes specific numbers, percentages, and data points gets cited 30-40% more often than content without them. Generative models favor claims they can attribute to a concrete source.
2. **Use authoritative language.** Fluency, technical precision, and confident assertions increase citation rates. Hedging and vague language reduce them.
3. **Add unique value.** Content that restates what every other page says gets deprioritized. Original analysis, proprietary data, and first-party experience signal that a source is worth citing.
4. **Structure for extraction.** Short paragraphs, clear headers, and definition-first formatting make it easier for models to pull the relevant passage.

GEO overlaps with AEO but is more specifically focused on the mechanics of generative models -- how they select, weight, and attribute sources during answer synthesis.

## Why It Matters for B2B Teams

The shift from link-based to generative search is accelerating. When a B2B buyer searches "best tools for identifying website visitors," they increasingly get a generated answer with 3-5 cited sources -- not a page of 10 blue links. Winning a citation in that generated answer is the new equivalent of ranking on page one.

For B2B content teams, GEO means investing in data-rich, authoritative content that generative models recognize as a primary source on a topic.

## How It Relates to MidBound

B2B teams evaluating visitor identification tools often use generative search to compare options. Content that clearly explains the difference between person-level and company-level identification -- with specific metrics and use cases -- is more likely to be cited by generative engines, driving awareness to solutions like MidBound at the moment of research.
