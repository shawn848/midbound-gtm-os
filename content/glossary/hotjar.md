Hotjar is a behavior analytics and user feedback tool that uses heatmaps, session recordings, feedback widgets, and surveys to show how users interact with a website -- revealing what they click, where they scroll, and where they drop off.

## How It Works

Hotjar provides qualitative insights that complement quantitative analytics tools like GA4:

**Heatmaps.** Visual overlays showing where users click, move their cursor, and scroll on a page. Click heatmaps reveal which elements get the most interaction. Scroll heatmaps show what percentage of visitors reach each section of the page. This helps identify whether key content (pricing, CTAs, product features) is actually being seen and engaged with.

**Session recordings.** Video replays of individual user sessions showing exactly how a visitor navigated the site -- where they hesitated, what they clicked, where they got confused, and when they left. These recordings surface UX issues that aggregate analytics cannot.

**Feedback widgets.** On-page tools that let visitors flag issues or rate their experience. Useful for catching problems on specific pages (broken forms, confusing navigation, missing information).

**Surveys.** Targeted questionnaires that appear based on user behavior (e.g., exit intent, time on page). These collect direct feedback about visitor intent and satisfaction.

## Why It Matters for B2B Teams

GA4 tells you what happened (500 people visited the pricing page, 3% converted). Hotjar tells you why. B2B teams use Hotjar to understand why visitors are not converting -- are they not scrolling to the CTA? Are they clicking on elements that are not clickable? Are they leaving immediately after seeing the pricing?

For B2B websites with complex sales processes, understanding visitor behavior at this level helps optimize pages for higher conversion rates.

## How It Relates to MidBound

Hotjar shows how visitors behave. MidBound shows who visitors are. Hotjar reveals that someone spent 4 minutes on the pricing page and scrolled through every section. MidBound reveals that the someone was a VP of Marketing at a target account. Together, they give B2B teams both behavioral context and individual identity -- the full picture of who is interested and how engaged they are.
