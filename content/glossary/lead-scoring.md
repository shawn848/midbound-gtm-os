Lead scoring is a methodology for ranking prospects based on how likely they are to become customers. It assigns numerical values to leads based on two dimensions: who they are (demographic and firmographic fit) and what they have done (behavioral signals). Sales teams use lead scores to prioritize outreach, focusing effort on the leads most likely to close.

## How It Works

Lead scoring models typically combine two types of criteria:

**ICP-based scoring (fit)** evaluates whether a lead matches your ideal customer profile:
- Job title and seniority (e.g., VP of Marketing = +20 points, Intern = -10 points)
- Company size and revenue (e.g., 50-500 employees = +15 points)
- Industry vertical (e.g., B2B SaaS = +10 points)
- Geographic location (e.g., US-based = +10 points)

**Behavior-based scoring (intent)** evaluates engagement levels:
- Pricing page visit = +25 points
- Case study viewed = +15 points
- Blog post read = +5 points
- Multiple visits in one week = +20 points
- Comparison page viewed = +20 points

Most B2B teams set a threshold (e.g., 50 points) that triggers a sales action, whether that is a personalized email, a phone call, or routing to an account executive. Leads below the threshold stay in marketing nurture sequences.

The challenge with traditional lead scoring is that it only works on known contacts. If someone fills out a form with a generic title or fake email, the scoring breaks. More fundamentally, the 95-98% of visitors who never fill out a form are invisible to the scoring model entirely.
