Structured data is code added to a webpage that describes its content in a format machines can parse directly. The most common implementation uses JSON-LD (JavaScript Object Notation for Linked Data) with vocabulary from schema.org, a collaborative standard maintained by Google, Microsoft, Yahoo, and Yandex.

## Why It Matters

Search engines can read your page's text, but they cannot always infer what that text represents. Structured data removes the ambiguity. It tells crawlers explicitly that a block of text is a product description, a FAQ answer, an author bio, or a glossary definition.

For traditional search, structured data enables rich results: star ratings, FAQ dropdowns, pricing information, and breadcrumb navigation displayed directly in the SERP. Pages with rich results consistently earn higher click-through rates than plain blue links.

For AI answer engines, structured data serves a different but equally important function. When ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google AI Overviews synthesize answers from multiple sources, they need to understand the relationship between content elements. Structured data provides those signals:

- **DefinedTerm** schema tells AI models that a page contains an authoritative definition.
- **FAQPage** schema marks content as question-and-answer pairs ready for extraction.
- **Article** schema identifies authorship, publication date, and topic.
- **Organization** schema links content to a specific company and its properties.

Without structured data, your content competes purely on text relevance. With it, you give AI models explicit metadata that increases your likelihood of being selected as a citation source.
