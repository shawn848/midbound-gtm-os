A topic cluster is a content architecture where a comprehensive pillar page covers a broad subject and links to multiple related cluster pages that address specific subtopics in depth. The cluster pages link back to the pillar, creating a tightly interconnected content hub that signals topical authority to search engines and AI models.

## Why It Matters

Traditional keyword-focused SEO treats each page as an independent asset targeting a single query. Topic clusters take a different approach. They build depth around an entire subject area, which is how both search algorithms and AI models evaluate expertise.

A typical topic cluster structure looks like this:

- **Pillar page**: A comprehensive guide (e.g., "The Complete Guide to Website Visitor Identification") covering the topic broadly in 2,000-4,000 words.
- **Cluster pages**: 8-15 focused pages addressing specific angles (e.g., "Person-Level vs. Company-Level Identification," "How Deanonymization Works," "Visitor Identification for Demand Gen Agencies").
- **Internal links**: Every cluster page links to the pillar. The pillar links to every cluster page. Cluster pages cross-link where relevant.

This structure helps search engines understand that your site has comprehensive coverage of a topic, not just a single page that mentions a keyword. For AI answer engines, topic clusters are even more important. AI models assess topical depth when deciding which sources to cite. A site with 15 interconnected pages about visitor identification is more likely to be cited than a site with one blog post on the subject.

The internal linking also distributes page authority. When one cluster page earns backlinks, that authority flows through internal links to the pillar and other cluster pages.

## How It Relates to MidBound

Topic clusters drive more organic and AI-referred traffic across multiple related queries, not just one keyword. For B2B companies using MidBound, this means more visitors landing on different pages throughout their research process. MidBound identifies each of those visitors at the person level, so you can see when the same buyer visits your pillar page on Monday, a comparison page on Wednesday, and a pricing page on Friday. That multi-page visit pattern reveals buying intent that a single pageview would miss.
