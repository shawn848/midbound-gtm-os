UTM parameters are standardized URL tags (source, medium, campaign, term, content) appended to links that track which marketing campaigns, channels, and creative variants drive traffic to your website -- forming the foundation of digital marketing attribution.

## How It Works

When you share a link with UTM parameters, anyone who clicks it carries those tags into your analytics platform. The five standard UTM parameters are:

- **utm_source** -- Where the traffic came from (linkedin, google, newsletter)
- **utm_medium** -- The marketing channel (paid, organic, email, social)
- **utm_campaign** -- The specific campaign name (q2-abm, product-launch)
- **utm_term** -- The search keyword (for paid search campaigns)
- **utm_content** -- The specific ad or link variant (for A/B testing)

Analytics tools read these tags and attribute the visit to the specified source, medium, and campaign.

## Why It Matters for B2B Teams

UTM parameters answer the question "which campaigns are actually working?" The limitation in traditional analytics is that they report at the aggregate level -- "47 clicks from the Q2 ABM campaign" -- but not who those 47 people were.

## How It Relates to MidBound

MidBound captures UTM parameters alongside visitor identification. When someone clicks your tagged LinkedIn ad and visits your site, MidBound identifies them by name and ties the visit to the specific UTM campaign. Instead of "47 clicks," you report "47 identified visitors including 12 ICP matches, and here are their names." That is person-level campaign attribution.
