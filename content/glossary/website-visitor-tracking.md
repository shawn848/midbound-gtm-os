Website visitor tracking is the practice of collecting and analyzing data on how visitors interact with a website -- including pages viewed, time spent per page, navigation paths, referral sources, form interactions, and other behavioral signals.

## How It Works

Website visitor tracking is implemented through JavaScript snippets, pixels, or server-side logging that capture visitor interactions as they occur.

Common data points collected:

- **Pages viewed.** Which URLs the visitor accessed during their session.
- **Time on page.** How long the visitor spent on each page before navigating away.
- **Session duration.** Total time from first page load to last interaction.
- **Referral source.** How the visitor arrived -- organic search, paid ad, social media, direct, email link.
- **UTM parameters.** Campaign, source, medium, and content tags attached to the URL.
- **Scroll depth.** How far down the page the visitor scrolled.
- **Click patterns.** Which elements the visitor clicked on.
- **Device and location.** Browser, operating system, screen size, and geographic location.

Most tracking is implemented through tools like Google Analytics (aggregate behavioral analytics), Hotjar (heatmaps and session recordings), and specialized tools that capture specific events or interactions.

The data is typically anonymous -- tracking systems assign a random session ID but do not know the visitor's name, company, or contact information.

## Why It Matters for B2B Teams

Visitor tracking data tells B2B teams which content resonates, where visitors drop off, and which marketing channels drive the most engaged traffic. It informs decisions about content strategy, site architecture, and conversion optimization.

For sales teams, behavioral tracking data becomes especially valuable when combined with identity resolution. Knowing that a visitor spent 5 minutes on the pricing page is interesting. Knowing that a VP of Marketing at a target account spent 5 minutes on the pricing page is actionable.

## How It Relates to MidBound

MidBound combines visitor tracking with person-level identification. It captures behavioral data -- pages viewed, time on site, session behavior -- and attaches it to the identified individual. This means sales reps do not just see a name and title; they see exactly what that person looked at and how engaged they were, providing context for personalized outreach.
