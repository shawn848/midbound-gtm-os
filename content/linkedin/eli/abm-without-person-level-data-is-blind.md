ABM was supposed to fix outbound. It made it more organized. It didn't make it accurate.

Your team identifies a target account. Finds 4-5 contacts by title. Runs sequences for two weeks. Two opens. One "not the right person." Three ghosts.

Meanwhile, someone from that same account visited your pricing page three times last week.

But your ABM tools don't tell you who. Maybe they tell you the company. "Someone from Snowflake visited." Snowflake has thousands of employees.

The person who was actually on your site never gets contacted. Your SDR is busy following up with the 4 LinkedIn picks who weren't interested.

Real story from a customer POC.

They'd been working a target account for months. Sequences on the VP of Sales and Director of Marketing. Normal playbook.

MidBound surfaced an analyst from that account on their pricing page. Analysts weren't in their ICP. The AE reached out anyway.

That analyst was leading the internal evaluation. The VP had delegated the entire buying process.

$30K closed deal.

ABM gave them the logo. They'd had it on their list for months.

MidBound gave them the deal. It surfaced the person who was actually buying.

Here's the math:

→ Account-level targeting: useful but incomplete
→ Company-level identification: slightly more useful, still vague
→ Person-level identification: actionable

Accounts don't buy. People do.

Prove me wrong.
