I spent a few weeks in NYC, SF, and LA. 100+ meetings with VCs, angels, and founders.

The real question in every room was the same.

Can this be absorbed? Can a bigger company build this in 3 months?

If you can't answer that with something concrete, the meeting ends politely and nothing happens.

The most consistent advice from investors who actually engaged deeply:

Go vertical. Win deeply in one segment. Become the default in that niche before you expand.

This sounds obvious. It's incredibly hard to practice. When you have a horizontal product, the temptation is to serve everyone. That temptation is a trap.

10 customers who can't live without you is worth more than 100 who kind of like you.

Here's the test I use now. Would this customer be angry if we disappeared? Not inconvenienced. Angry. Would they try to talk us out of shutting down?

If yes, you've won deeply. If they'd shrug and find an alternative, you haven't.

The other thing that stuck: proprietary data is the moat that's hardest to replicate. Code can be rebuilt. Features can be copied. Speed to market is temporary.

But a dataset that improves with every customer and compounds over time? That's structural.

Velocity is a moat too. A team that ships 2x faster doesn't just get to market sooner. They learn 2x faster. They course-correct 2x faster. Over 18 months that compounds into something a big company can't match.

Go vertical. Win deeply. Build the data flywheel. Move fast. Then expand.

I should have opened a daycare in Minnesota. Would have been simpler. But this is more fun.
