Most people think sales is about control. It's not.

Imagine you go to a doctor. You start explaining what hurts. Thirty seconds in, they cut you off, pull out a prescription pad, and write a script.

Would you trust that doctor?

That's how most sales calls go. Prospect says "outbound." Rep hears the keyword and spends 20 minutes pitching features. Prospect nods politely. "We'll think about it." Ghosted.

The rep controlled the conversation. They just didn't diagnose the problem.

Three things good doctors do that good reps should steal:

1. Create space for honest answers. Ask open questions. Shut up and listen. When a prospect feels heard, they'll share the political dynamics, the competing priorities, the reason the last vendor failed. That's diagnostic gold.

2. Assume forward progress without pushing. Not "would you like a proposal?" but "I'll send a recommendation Thursday and we'll walk through it Friday." Buyers respond to confidence that doesn't feel pushy.

3. Lean into hard questions early. "Is budget approved or are we exploratory?" "Who else weighs in?" Deals built on avoided questions collapse during procurement.

Deals don't follow a selling process. They follow a buying process.

Your job isn't to control the conversation. It's to understand where the buyer is and help them get to the next step.

Everything else is noise.
