What is success as a founder?

Raise a big round. Get featured in TechCrunch. Hire 50 people.

That's the costume version. Looks good. Hollow underneath.

Founder A raises $10M. Posts about it. 3,000 likes. Six months later they're burning $800K/month on $200K revenue. Twelve months later they're doing a bridge.

Founder B. Bootstrapped. No press. $3M ARR. 80% margins. Team of 8. Nobody writes about them.

Which one is more successful?

Revenue is vanity. Profit is sanity.

$5M ARR sounds incredible until you learn the company spends $6M to generate it. That's not a business. That's a money transfer operation with a SaaS wrapper.

We built MidBound to six figures in five months. Three people. $0 CAC.

I don't say that to brag. I say it because the startup world has this obsession with headcount and raise size as signs of progress.

Spending more money faster is not a sign of progress.

The four metrics I actually care about:

EBITDA. Not revenue.
Net revenue retention. Are customers paying more over time?
Customer dependency. Would they fight to keep you?
Cash relative to burn. How many months can you survive?

Everything else is decoration.

Are you building a business or building a story?
