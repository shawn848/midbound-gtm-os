- Also active on X (@RetentionAdam) and runs the RB2B Newsletter on Beehiiv

### Recent Post Themes (Last 90 Days)

**Theme 1: Build in Public / Revenue Transparency**
- Publicly shares ARR milestones, churn numbers, team size, and failures
- Posted about "the impossible just happened at RB2B" (likely a product or revenue milestone)
- Previously declared RB2B "dead" in Nov 2024 when churn outpaced new revenue, then posted the recovery arc
- This transparency IS the marketing -- his target audience is B2B founders/marketers who root for his story

**Theme 2: Founder-Led Growth as the Only Channel**

- Core thesis: LinkedIn audience took RB2B from $0 to $6M ARR in 18 months as the sole distribution channel
- Predicted more founders will build in public past $100M ARR

**Theme 3: "Inbound-Led Outbound" as the New GTM Motion**
- Positions a hybrid model: use inbound content to generate warm signals, then layer outbound on top
- Claims this converts at 10x traditional cold email
- RB2B itself is both the proof case (grew via his LinkedIn) and the tool enabling this motion for customers

**Theme 4: Category Definition -- Contact-Based Marketing**
- Actively defining and naming the category he operates in
- Uses "contact-based marketing" (CBM) as the evolution beyond ABM
- Frames it as: ABM targets accounts, CBM targets people within those accounts

**Theme 5: Anti-Establishment SaaS Takes**
- Provocative opinions on bootstrapping vs VC, traditional enterprise sales being dead, and bloated SaaS teams
- "The future is 10-person, $100M companies" is a recurring thesis
- Uses polarization strategically -- some people love it, others hate it, algorithm rewards both

### Content Strategy Breakdown

**Posting frequency:** ~36 posts/month (roughly daily plus extras)

**Content pillars:**
1. Build in public (real revenue numbers, wins and failures)
2. The future of GTM (inbound-led outbound, founder brand > demand gen)
3. Bootstrapping vs VC (provocative anti-VC takes)
4. Founder psychology (vulnerability, anxiety, embarrassment stories)

**The "Prolific Zone" concept:** Content that's authentic to the creator, not mainstream, but not so extreme people dismiss it. The sweet spot where some love you and others hate you -- perfect for algorithm engagement.

**Engagement patterns:**
- Hook-first writing: "A mediocre post with a great hook outperforms a great post with a weak one"
- Only 1 in 10 posts has a hard CTA -- the rest are stories
- Revenue numbers and money metrics drive highest engagement
- Vulnerability posts (embarrassment, failure) get strong emotional engagement
- Cease-and-desist story generated 1,600 likes, 913 comments, 1,600 leads from a single post

**Strategy shift:** Initially used ghostwriters and viral bait ("Do you agree?" screenshot quotes), now focuses on authentic storytelling. Prioritizes "buying audience" over follower count (Nathan Barry's "audience vs crowd" distinction).

### Positioning Language

- "Person-level identification" (technical)
- "Contact-based marketing" (category name)
- "The old model stops at the company" (competitive framing)
- "Company-level ID is an educated guess, not intelligence" (attack vector)
- Frames company-level tools as "lottery tickets with better branding"
- Uses "earned attention" vs "bought attention" for his marketing philosophy

### Product Claims

- Person-level identification of website visitors (name, title, company, validated email, LinkedIn profile)
- 40-45% contact-level identification rate for US traffic (RB2B's own stated number)
- With Demandbase partnership: 70-80% total identification (person + company combined)
- US-only for person-level ID (geofencing for GDPR compliance)
- Global company-level ID through Demandbase integration
- Free tier exists but person-level was removed from free plan in January 2026 (now company-level only on free)
- Paid plans start at $149/month (Pro, 300 credits)
- Integration with Clay, Apollo, Instantly, Zapier, Smartlead, Slack
- "Hot lead" and page tagging for custom segmentation

### The Demandbase Partnership (May 2025)

This is strategically significant. RB2B partnered with Demandbase to combine person-level + company-level ID. Every paying RB2B customer gets unlimited Demandbase company-level resolution included. This directly neutralizes the "RB2B only does person-level" attack and extends their coverage to 70-80% of total traffic.

Implications for MidBound: RB2B is no longer a pure-play person-level tool. They now offer both layers. Any competitor positioning against RB2B as "we do both person and company" needs to account for this partnership.

### How He Talks About Competitors

- Names 6sense, Demandbase, Clearbit directly and dismissively
- Frames them as "the old model" that stops at company-level
- Partnered with Demandbase rather than competing -- smart flanking move
- Does NOT typically name smaller person-level competitors (Vector, Warmly, etc.) by name in organic posts
- Has a dedicated comparison page on rb2b.com (rb2b.com/compare) that does name and position against Vector specifically

### The Category Battle

Robinson is trying to OWN the term "contact-based marketing" as the category name. He coined it (or at least popularized it) and positions RB2B as the category creator.

Vector uses "contact-level advertising" -- subtly different. "Advertising" signals they're an ad platform, not just an identification tool. This is strategic: it positions them in marketing budgets, not sales tool budgets.

MidBound opportunity: Neither "contact-based marketing" nor "contact-level advertising" has been definitively claimed. The space is still naming itself. "Person-level identification" remains the most neutral, technically descriptive term. MidBound could either adopt one of these, or create its own category language. The term "person-level" aligns with MidBound's existing language and avoids ceding category ownership to Robinson or Perk.

## MATCH RATE REALITY CHECK

### What Vendors Claim vs What Independent Testing Shows

| Metric | Vendor Claims | Independent Reality |
|--------|--------------|-------------------|
| Company-level match rate | 60-80% | 30-65% (varies heavily by traffic source) |
| Person-level match rate | 40-80% (varies by vendor) | 5-20% for most tools |
| RB2B person-level (their claim) | 40-45% | 5-20% per independent tests (Leadpipe review) |
| RB2B total with Demandbase | 70-80% | Blends person + company; person portion is lower |
| Vector enrichment rate | 25% (on their own comparison page) | Not independently verified |

### The Match Rate Inflation Problem

- Most vendors claiming 80%+ are counting company-level matches, not person-level
- Enterprise office workers: 50-65% company identification possible
- SMB remote workers: 5-10% (home IP = ISP, not employer)
- International traffic: 15-30% company-level
- 60%+ of knowledge workers are remote/hybrid in 2026 -- this structurally limits match rates

### Content Opportunity for MidBound

The match rate conversation is dominated by inflated vendor claims. An honest, technically grounded breakdown of "what match rates actually mean" -- separating company-level from person-level, explaining the remote work impact, showing how traffic source affects rates -- would be high-value content. Nobody in the space is doing this transparently.

## PRIVACY AND ETHICAL DEBATES

### Current State of the Debate

- All person-level tools are US-only for person-level ID (GDPR prevents it in EU)
- RB2B uses geofencing: if IP can't be confirmed as US, they fall back to company-level only
- CCPA/CPRA compliance is claimed by all players (opt-out mechanisms, "Do Not Sell" links)
- Legal basis for B2B use: "legitimate interest" -- business has reason to know who visits their website
- 19 US states now have comprehensive data protection laws through 2026
- Business contact data is fully protected in California (exemption ended 2023)

### How Robinson Handles Privacy

- Doesn't dwell on it in organic content
- Relies on "we're CCPA compliant" and geofencing as the defense
- Uses "it's not tracking people who don't want to be tracked, it's identifying professional visitors on business websites" framing
- His content avoids the privacy debate rather than engaging it head-on

### How Perk/Vector Handles Privacy

- Similar avoidance strategy
- Emphasizes "verified contacts" and "ICP matching" to frame it as precision, not surveillance
- Less public discussion of the privacy debate than Robinson

### Content Opportunity for MidBound

Eli's framing ("It's not invasive; it's responsive. It's not cold; it's warm") is more thoughtful than either competitor's approach. The privacy conversation is being avoided by both Robinson and Perk rather than addressed. A measured, honest exploration of the ethics -- written by a technical co-founder who can speak to the how -- would differentiate MidBound.

## CONTENT GAPS: WHAT NEITHER PERSON IS COVERING

### Gap 1: Honest Match Rate Education
Nobody in the space is transparently explaining what match rates really mean, how they vary by traffic source, and why vendor claims diverge from reality. This is the highest-value content gap.

### Gap 2: Implementation Playbooks Beyond "We Push to Slack"
Both Robinson and Perk focus on the identification moment. Neither publishes deep content about what happens AFTER identification -- the actual outreach sequences, prioritization frameworks, multi-stakeholder play execution, or how to avoid burning identified leads with bad outreach.

### Gap 3: Technical Depth on How De-anonymization Actually Works
Both stay surface-level on the technology. Robinson uses "browser fingerprinting, IP lookup, and LinkedIn matching" as a hand-wave. Neither explains deterministic vs probabilistic matching, identity graphs, cookie mechanics, or signal layering. Technical buyers want this depth.

### Gap 4: Multi-Stakeholder Buying Committee Intelligence
Both mention "see who's visiting" but neither produces content about using visitor identification to map buying committees in real-time. The "3 people from the same company visited this week" insight is powerful but under-explored in their content.

### Gap 5: The "What If Your Match Rate Is Low?" Conversation
Neither addresses what you do when your traffic composition yields low match rates (heavy international traffic, SMB remote workers, etc.). Content about optimizing for identifiability -- which traffic sources yield highest match rates, how to improve your identifiable traffic percentage -- doesn't exist.

### Gap 6: Privacy-Thoughtful Positioning
Both dodge the privacy question. Neither has published a serious, balanced treatment of the ethics of de-anonymization. There's space for a "here's how we think about this responsibly" position.

### Gap 7: Agency and Consultant Use Cases
Both primarily target in-house B2B teams. Demand gen agencies, RevOps consultants, and fractional CMOs who manage multiple client sites are an underserved audience in the content.

### Gap 8: Cost-Per-Identified-Visitor Economics
Nobody is publishing transparent unit economics: what does it actually cost per actionable identified visitor when you factor in match rates, credit consumption, and conversion rates? This would help buyers make informed decisions.

### Gap 9: Integration Depth Beyond "We Integrate with X"
Both list integrations but neither shows the actual workflows. "Here's the exact HubSpot workflow we built to route identified visitors to the right rep based on ICP score and page behavior" would be differentiated content.

### Gap 10: Non-US Market Reality
Both are US-only for person-level ID. Neither addresses the reality for global businesses. Content about "what works in Europe vs US" and how to build a visitor intelligence strategy that accounts for GDPR would serve an underserved audience.

## COMPETITIVE POSITIONING IMPLICATIONS FOR MIDBOUND

### Critical Blind Spot in Current MidBound Competitive Materials

MidBound's current competitor-landscape.md and competitors.md mention ONLY company-level tools (Snitcher, Factors, Clearbit, 6sense, Demandbase). RB2B and Vector are not mentioned at all. This is a significant gap -- RB2B is the most visible person-level competitor and is the company most MidBound prospects will have heard of.

### Positioning Against RB2B

RB2B's strengths to acknowledge:
- Larger brand awareness (Robinson's 125K LinkedIn following is a moat)
- Demandbase partnership gives them company + person coverage
- Lower entry price ($149/mo vs MidBound pricing)
- Strong Slack integration that customers love
- "Build in public" story gives them credibility

RB2B's vulnerabilities to exploit:
- Free tier gutted in Jan 2026 (person-level removed from free plan) -- created user frustration
- Credit-based model means high-traffic sites burn through credits on irrelevant visitors
- Match rate claims (40-45%) are disputed by independent testing (5-20%)
- US-only for person-level
- Heavy dependency on Robinson's personal brand -- what happens if he steps back?
- Churn was a real problem they acknowledged publicly (declared "dead" Nov 2024)

### Positioning Against Vector

Vector's strengths to acknowledge:
- Y Combinator backing provides credibility
- Off-site intent is a genuine differentiator (OffsiteID)
- Ad audience activation across LinkedIn, Google, Meta is compelling
- Strong brand execution (Ghostie, events, podcast)
- Jess Cook's marketing is excellent

Vector's vulnerabilities to exploit:
- Higher price point ($399/mo minimum, $4,500/quarter)
- Lower enrichment rate (25% per their own comparison page)
- Smaller brand awareness than RB2B
- "Contact-level advertising" positioning limits them to marketing budgets -- sales teams may not see them as relevant
- Off-site intent claims are hard to verify and may not perform as advertised (Robinson attacks this directly)

### MidBound's Potential Differentiation

Given both competitors' positioning, MidBound should consider:

1. **Technical credibility** -- Neither Robinson nor Perk goes deep on HOW the technology works. If Eli can explain the technical mechanics in an accessible way, this builds trust that vendor-speak erodes.

2. **Honest match rate conversation** -- Be the first company to publish transparent, caveated match rates with methodology. "Here's what we see across X,000 visitors, broken down by traffic source."

3. **Post-identification workflow depth** -- Own the "what happens after you identify someone" conversation. Outreach sequences, prioritization frameworks, multi-stakeholder plays.

4. **Privacy-forward positioning** -- Eli's framing is more thoughtful than either competitor's dismissive approach. Lean into this as a brand differentiator.

5. **The agency/consultant angle** -- Neither competitor targets multi-client users. This is a white space.

6. **Speed and simplicity** -- Robinson built RB2B's early growth on simplicity. If MidBound can match that ease while delivering better output, that's a compelling story.
