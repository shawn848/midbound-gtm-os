# Messaging Angles

5 core messaging angles, each tied to a content pillar. Use the right angle for the right audience and context.

---

## Angle 1: "Company-Level Is Dead" (Tied to: Contrarian Takes)

**Core message:** The entire industry built around telling you which company visited your site is solving the wrong problem. Person-level is the future.

**When to use:**
- Competitive displacement conversations
- LinkedIn posts targeting demand gen leaders
- Cold outreach to companies currently using Snitcher, Factors, or Clearbit
- Conference talks or webinar content

**Key lines:**
- "Every other tool in the space tells you which company visited. We tell you which person."
- "Company-level ID is a scam." (Sebastian's aggressive version)
- "Accounts don't buy. People do." (Eli's strategic version)

**Supporting proof:**
- 97% of visitors leave anonymous with company-level tools
- Knowing "Acme Corp visited" with 2,000 employees is not actionable
- MidBound: 6 figures in 5 months, $0 CAC (market agrees)

## Angle 2: "The 97% Problem" (Tied to: Product Education)

**Core message:** 97% of your website visitors leave without converting. You're paying to drive traffic you can't see. Person-level ID recovers that invisible pipeline.

**When to use:**
- Demand gen audience (they feel this pain daily)
- Ad spend ROI conversations
- Marketing leader pitch
- Blog SEO content (high search intent for "website visitor identification")

**Key lines:**
- "97% of your visitors are ghosts. You paid to bring them in. You just can't see who they are."
- "You're spending $50K/month on ads with zero clue who visited." (Sebastian)
- "Imagine you throw a party. 100 people show up. 97 leave without saying a word." (Eli)

**Supporting proof:**
- Average website conversion rate: 2-3%
- $30K/month ad spend example: 2,000 clicks, 60 form fills vs. 400 identified visitors
- The math: what's 10% of your invisible visitors worth to pipeline?

## Angle 3: "ABM to PBM" (Tied to: ABM Evolution)

**Core message:** Account-based marketing was a step forward. Person-based marketing is the next one. ABM targets buildings. PBM targets people.

**When to use:**
- ABM team conversations
- Companies already running 6sense or Demandbase (complement positioning)
- Industry thought leadership content
- Eli's LinkedIn and blog content

**Key lines:**
- "ABM is sending a letter to a building. PBM is sending a letter to a person." (Eli)
- "You have 200 target accounts. 40 show intent. Now which person at each account do you call?" (Eli)
- "Your ABM stack needs a person-level upgrade." (Sebastian)

**Supporting proof:**
- Multi-stakeholder detection shows the buying committee forming
- Person-level data turns account signals into actionable outreach
- Speed to lead: know who to contact before the research step

## Angle 4: "Built from Zero" (Tied to: Origin Stories + Founder Lessons)

**Core message:** Two founders. Zero network. Zero paid marketing. $70K friends and family. Two pivots. Then 6 figures in 5 months. The story is the proof.

**When to use:**
- Founder audience (r/startups, r/SaaS, founder communities)
- VC conversations and investor updates
- Brand-building content (humanizes the company)
- Recruitment and hiring content

**Key lines:**
- "I cold called 10 founders a day for 3 months." (Sebastian)
- "We pivoted twice before the market told us what to build." (Both)
- "Comfort didn't make me. Pressure did." (Sebastian)
- "Should have opened a daycare in Minnesota by this time." (Eli)

**Supporting proof:**
- $0 CAC to 6 figures in 5 months
- Team of 3
- Fusion VC batch 16
- Two pivots (auto-prospecting, AISDR) before finding PMF with deanonymization

## Angle 5: "Speed to Lead" (Tied to: Product Education + Use Cases)

**Core message:** The fastest follow-up wins. MidBound identifies visitors in real-time so your team can reach out the same day, with context, while your product is still on the visitor's mind.

**When to use:**
- Sales leader conversations
- Speed-to-lead competitive context
- Slack integration pitch
- Outbound teams looking for warm signals

**Key lines:**
- "Your website visitor was on your pricing page 20 minutes ago. Your rep already has a Slack notification with their name, title, and LinkedIn. That's speed to lead."
- "The difference between reaching out today and reaching out next week is the difference between warm and cold."
- "Stop guessing. Start knowing." (Sebastian)

**Supporting proof:**
- Harvard Business Review: companies that contact leads within an hour are 7x more likely to qualify
- Real-time Slack alerts mean minutes, not days
- Outreach with visit context converts at significantly higher rates than cold
