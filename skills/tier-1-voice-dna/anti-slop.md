# Anti-AI-Slop Patterns -- MidBound

These patterns make content sound AI-generated. Check every draft against this list. If 3 or more patterns are flagged, rewrite the entire draft from scratch. Do not patch individual phrases.

## Always Catch (Critical)

1. **Em-dashes** (---): Delete all. Use periods, commas, or restructure the sentence. Neither Sebastian nor Eli uses em-dashes naturally.

2. **Authority Signaling Phrases**: "The uncomfortable truth", "Let me be clear", "Here's what nobody tells you", "The hard truth is", "Here's the reality", "What most people miss is..."
   - Sebastian's version of authority is direct claims, not performative setups: "Company-level ID is a scam." Not: "Here's the hard truth about company-level ID."
   - Eli's version is structured argument, not dramatic framing: "Here's the math:" is directional, not performative. That one is fine.

3. **Narrator Setup Lines**: "Here's the thing about...", "Here's where it gets interesting..." Delete the setup. Start with the actual point.

4. **Dramatic Rhetorical Framing**: "But here's the part where...", "And that's when it clicked.", "Want to know the crazy part?" State what happened. Let the reader draw the conclusion.

5. **Three Parallel Dramatic Sentences**: "You can't see it. You can't measure it. You can't ignore it." Cut to one direct statement. Sebastian uses single-line punches, not triple parallel structures.

6. **The Bookend Summary**: Opening with a thesis and closing by restating the exact same thesis in different words. Real posts go somewhere new by the end. Sebastian ends with a command. Eli ends with "Prove me wrong."

7. **Self-Branded Concepts**: "This is what I call..." or "I like to call this..." Just explain the concept. Neither founder brands their ideas with catchy names.

8. **Artificial Drama Sentences**: "The shift sounds simple. It's not." Show why it's hard with a specific example instead.

9. **Colon-Listed Everything**: "the result: better data. the impact: faster sales." Write natural sentences. Exception: Eli uses "Here's the math:" as a natural transition into numbers, and that's fine because it serves a structural purpose.

10. **The Humble Brag Disclaimer**: "I don't have all the answers, but..." or "I'm no expert, but..." Sebastian would never disclaim. Eli handles nuance with "Don't get me wrong though..." which is different -- it's adding complexity, not false humility.

11. **Hype Words**: "game changer", "revolutionary", "unleash", "supercharge", "next-level", "paradigm shift", "disruptive." Replace with what actually changed or happened. "MidBound identifies the actual person" is stronger than "MidBound is a game changer."

12. **"No fluff" / "No BS" Disclaimers**: Saying "no fluff" is itself fluff. If the content has substance, the reader will know.

13. **Transition Padding**: "With that being said...", "That said...", "Now, let's talk about...", "Moving on to..." Just make the next point. Whitespace is the transition.

14. **Generic Value Propositions**: "Save time and money", "Scale your business", "Take your GTM to the next level." Be specific about what MidBound does: "Know exactly who visited your pricing page. Name, title, email. In real-time."

15. **Engagement Bait Closers**: "What do you think?", "Drop your thoughts below!", "Comment if you agree!" Sebastian ends with commands. Eli ends with "Prove me wrong." Neither begs for engagement.

## Context-Dependent (Check but don't auto-delete)

16. **"You" + accusation**: Sebastian uses "you" aggressively and it's authentic: "You're spending $50K on ads and you don't know who visited." This is his voice. Only flag if the "you" sounds generic rather than targeted.

17. **Bullet lists for arguments**: Arguments belong in prose. BUT feature lists, integration descriptions, and comparison tables use bullets naturally. Bullets for execution and comparison, prose for reasoning.

18. **Bold for emphasis**: Neither founder uses bold text heavily in social posts. Acceptable in blog posts for scanability. In LinkedIn and X posts, structure comes from whitespace and line breaks, not bold text.

19. **Rhetorical questions**: Sebastian uses accusatory rhetorical questions naturally: "You're spending $50K/month on ads and you don't know who visited?" This is fine. Flag only when the rhetorical question is a generic setup like "Ever wonder why your pipeline is dry?"

## Sebastian's Natural Patterns (Not Slop)

These look like AI patterns but are actually Sebastian's voice. Do not flag:

- Single-line declarative sentences as standalone paragraphs
- Naming competitors directly: "Snitcher. Factors. All of them."
- "true story" as a transition into anecdotes
- Aggressive "you" address: "You're spending $50K/month on ads..."
- Numbers inline without context sentences: "$70K F&F round. 10 founders a day. 6 figures in 5 months."
- Commands as closers: "Stop reading, go do it."
- "zero" for emphasis: "zero clue", "$0 CAC"

## Eli's Natural Patterns (Not Slop)

These look like AI patterns but are actually Eli's voice. Do not flag:

- Extended analogies sustained across full posts (party, doctor, building)
- "Here's the math:" as a transition into revenue logic
- "Don't get me wrong though..." before balanced takes
- "Prove me wrong." as sign-off challenge
- Arrows for feature/action bullets
- Revenue numbers flowing through prose: "97% of visitors = 97% wasted ad spend"
- Self-deprecating humor: "should have opened a daycare in Minnesota by this time"
- Warm responses in comments -- genuine engagement, not performative
