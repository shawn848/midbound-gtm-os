# Blog Playbook -- MidBound

> Inherits from: `tier-1-voice-dna/sebastian-voice.md` and `tier-1-voice-dna/eli-voice.md`

## Platform DNA

The blog is the long-form home base. Blog posts serve three purposes:
1. SEO -- ranking for terms MidBound's audience searches
2. Authority -- substantive content that positions MidBound as the person-level ID leader
3. Repurposing -- blog posts feed LinkedIn, X, and Reddit content

## Frontmatter Schema

Every blog post starts with YAML frontmatter:

```yaml
---
title: "Post title here"
slug: "post-title-here"
author: "Sebastian Obadia" | "Eli Freedman"
date: "YYYY-MM-DD"
pillar: "contrarian-takes" | "origin-stories" | "product-education" | "abm-evolution" | "founder-lessons"
seo_title: "SEO-optimized title (60 chars max)"
seo_description: "Meta description (155 chars max)"
tags: ["deanonymization", "person-level-id", "abm", "gtm"]
status: "draft" | "review" | "published"
---
```

## Structure

### Blog Post Anatomy (800-1500 words)

1. **Hook (1-2 paragraphs):** The opening that earns the scroll. Same style as the founder's LinkedIn hooks but with slightly more room to breathe.

2. **The Problem (2-3 paragraphs):** What the reader is experiencing. Be specific. Use numbers and scenarios they recognize.

3. **The Insight (2-4 paragraphs):** The reframe, the data, the analogy. This is where Eli's extended analogies and Sebastian's contrarian claims live.

4. **The Solution (3-5 paragraphs):** How person-level identification addresses the problem. Specific MidBound features, use cases, or examples. Not a feature list -- a narrative that shows the before and after.

5. **The Evidence (1-3 paragraphs):** Numbers, results, or logical proof. "$0 CAC. 6 figures in 5 months. Team of 3." Or: "97% of visitors leave anonymous. Here's the math on what that costs you."

6. **The Close (1-2 paragraphs):** Forward-looking or action-oriented. Sebastian ends with a command. Eli ends with a thesis or challenge.

7. **CTA:** "Start your 14-day free trial. No credit card required." Link to midbound.ai/register.

## SEO Guidelines

### Title Optimization
- Include the primary keyword naturally
- 60 characters max for SEO title
- Blog title can be longer and more compelling
- Avoid clickbait -- the title should accurately describe the content

### Target Keywords by Pillar

**Contrarian Takes:**
- "company level vs person level identification"
- "website visitor identification"
- "deanonymization B2B"
- "better than Snitcher" / "Snitcher alternative"

**Product Education:**
- "identify website visitors by name"
- "person level website identification"
- "who visited my website"
- "website visitor identification tool"

**ABM Evolution:**
- "person based marketing"
- "ABM vs PBM"
- "account based marketing limitations"
- "next generation ABM"

**Origin Stories:**
- "B2B SaaS founder story"
- "startup pivot story"
- "building a SaaS company"

**Founder Lessons:**
- "B2B SaaS lessons"
- "startup growth lessons"
- "founder lessons learned"

### Meta Description
- 155 characters max
- Include primary keyword
- Communicate the value proposition
- Include a subtle call to action when natural

### Internal Linking
- Link to other blog posts in the same pillar
- Link to the product page when mentioning features
- Link to the registration page from the CTA
- Use descriptive anchor text, not "click here"

## Voice Adaptation for Blog

### Sebastian Blog Voice
Same DNA as LinkedIn but with slightly more room. Still punchy paragraphs. Still aggressive hooks. But 800-1500 words lets him build the argument with more evidence and detail. The blog version of Sebastian reads like a LinkedIn post that was allowed to breathe.

### Eli Blog Voice
The blog is Eli's best format. Extended analogies have room to develop fully. Revenue math can be shown step by step. The structured argument can build through multiple sections. The blog version of Eli reads like a well-structured essay from a CEO who actually thinks about these problems.

## Formatting Rules

- H1 for the title (one per post)
- H2 for major sections
- H3 for subsections within major sections
- Short paragraphs (2-4 sentences, not 1-2 like LinkedIn)
- Bold text for emphasis on key phrases (acceptable in blog, unlike LinkedIn)
- Bullet lists for features, integrations, comparisons (not for arguments)
- One image or diagram per post minimum (screenshot, comparison table, or flow diagram)
- No em-dashes
- No quotation marks around phrases
- Numbers as numerals

## Repurposing Flow

Each blog post generates:
1. **LinkedIn post** -- The hook + core insight, condensed to platform format
2. **X thread** -- The key points, one per tweet, 4-7 tweets
3. **Reddit post** -- The story or lesson, adapted for community norms

The blog is written first. Social is derived from the blog, not the other way around.

## Avoid

- Listicles disguised as blog posts ("10 Reasons to Use Person-Level ID")
- Generic introductions ("In today's fast-paced business environment...")
- Sections that exist just for SEO without adding value
- Keyword stuffing
- Over-optimizing for search at the expense of readability
- Posts under 800 words (too thin) or over 1500 words (too long unless the topic demands it)
