# LinkedIn Playbook -- MidBound

> Inherits from: `tier-1-voice-dna/sebastian-voice.md` and `tier-1-voice-dna/eli-voice.md`

## Tone

Shifts by founder. Load the right voice DNA before writing.

- **Sebastian:** Aggressive, direct, confrontational. Talks TO the reader. Punchy single-line format.
- **Eli:** Structured, analytical, analogy-driven. Invites the reader to think. Cleaner prose.

## Opening Line Style

- Always lowercase first word (unless proper noun or first-person I)
- Hook in first 2 lines. The "see more" cut happens fast on mobile -- the first line must earn the click.
- No generic greetings, no "I wanted to share..."

### Sebastian Openers

Lead with a bold claim, contradiction, or direct attack:
- "company-level ID is a scam. Snitcher. Factors. All of them."
- "marketers spending $50K/month on ads with zero clue who visited their site."
- "I see companies box their ICP so tight they kill their own pipeline."
- "most founders don't fail because of the market."

### Eli Openers

Lead with a reframe, analogy setup, or thesis:
- "imagine you throw a party. 100 people show up. 97 of them leave without saying a word."
- "accounts don't buy. People do."
- "97% of your website visitors leave without converting. That's 97% wasted ad spend."
- "we don't have a lead gen problem. We have a visibility problem."

## Paragraph Structure

### Sebastian
- 1-2 sentences per paragraph. Maximum.
- Heavy whitespace between every block
- Single-line punches as standalone paragraphs
- Reads like a text message, not an essay

### Eli
- 2-4 sentences per paragraph
- Whitespace between paragraphs but not as extreme
- Structured flow: setup, build, payoff
- Analogies sustain across 3-4 paragraphs

## Content Pillars

### Contrarian Takes (Sebastian primary)
Open with a provocative claim. Name names. Back it up with MidBound's results or personal experience. End with a command.

### Origin Stories (Sebastian primary)
Venezuela, Israel, combat unit, cold calling, pivots, F&F round. Raw and honest. Self-deprecating about the struggle. Absolute confidence about the product.

### Product Education (Both founders)
How MidBound works, what it does, specific use cases. Sebastian is aggressive ("you need this"). Eli is analytical ("here's what it means for your pipeline").

### ABM Evolution (Eli primary)
ABM to PBM thesis. Industry evolution. Extended analogies. Revenue math. "Prove me wrong."

### Founder Lessons (Both founders)
Personal growth, VC trip learnings, startup lessons. Sebastian is blunt and prescriptive. Eli is reflective and nuanced.

## CTA Style

### Sebastian
- "Stop reading. Go try it. midbound.ai/register"
- "14 days free. No credit card. No excuses."
- Ends with commands, not questions

### Eli
- "Prove me wrong."
- "Try it yourself. 14-day free trial, no credit card. midbound.ai/register"
- Ends with challenges or invitations, not demands

### Never
- "What do you think? Comment below!"
- "Agree? Hit that like button!"
- "Follow me for more tips!"
- "Drop a [emoji] if you agree"

## Comment Strategy

Both founders should engage in comments:
- **Sebastian:** Short, direct replies. One-line value adds. No generic "thanks for sharing."
- **Eli:** Warmer, more expansive replies. Genuinely engages with the commenter's point. Adds nuance or an additional angle.

## Formatting Rules

- No em-dashes
- No quotation marks around phrases
- Numbers as numerals (10, not ten; $50K, not fifty thousand)
- Competitor names capitalized: Snitcher, Factors, Clearbit
- No bold text in LinkedIn posts (use whitespace and line breaks for structure)
- No hashtags in the body. Zero to one at the very end, if any.

## Post Length

- Sebastian: 600-1200 characters. Punchy. No padding.
- Eli: 800-1800 characters. More room for analogies. Still no padding.
- Both: if the post can be shorter and still deliver value, make it shorter.

## Avoid

- Corporate speak ("we're thrilled to announce", "leveraging synergies")
- Generic B2B advice without MidBound specifics
- Authority signaling phrases (see anti-slop.md)
- Walls of text without whitespace
- Engagement bait without substance
- Em-dashes, quotation marks, hype words
