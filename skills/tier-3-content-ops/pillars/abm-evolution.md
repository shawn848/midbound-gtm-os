# Content Pillar: ABM Evolution

## Overview

The thesis that account-based marketing (ABM) is evolving into person-based marketing (PBM). This is Eli's signature pillar. The argument: ABM was a step forward from spray-and-pray marketing, but it stopped short. Knowing which accounts are in-market is valuable. Knowing which people at those accounts are actively engaging is transformative.

## Primary Founder

Eli Freedman. This is his strategic territory. He sees the industry arc and articulates where it's heading.

Sebastian can reference the ABM evolution in contrarian takes, but the thesis itself is Eli's.

## Format

### Eli ABM Evolution Post
1. **Thesis opener** (1-2 lines): State the evolution clearly. "ABM was a step forward. PBM is the next one."
2. **The history** (2-3 paragraphs): What ABM got right. Account targeting, intent signals, coordinated outreach. Give credit where due.
3. **The limitation** (2-3 paragraphs): What ABM gets wrong. Account-level data without person-level resolution. "You're marketing to a building, not a person."
4. **The evolution** (3-4 paragraphs): What person-based marketing looks like. How MidBound enables it. Specific examples or analogies.
5. **The forward look** (1-2 paragraphs): Where the industry is heading. What this means for marketing and sales teams.
6. **Sign-off**: "Prove me wrong." or a trial CTA.

## The ABM to PBM Thesis

### What ABM Got Right
- Targeting specific accounts instead of spraying the market
- Using intent signals to prioritize accounts
- Coordinating marketing and sales around shared account lists
- Moving from MQL to account-level engagement metrics

### Where ABM Falls Short
- Account-level identification shows a company, not a person
- "Acme Corp is showing intent" doesn't tell you who at Acme Corp to contact
- Multi-threading at target accounts requires knowing which individuals engaged
- ABM platforms are expensive and complex. Built for enterprises with dedicated ops teams.
- The gap between "this account is in-market" and "this person is ready to talk" is where deals die.

### What PBM Adds
- Person-level identification: know exactly who at the account is engaging
- Behavioral context: what they looked at, how long they spent, which pages
- Contact resolution: validated email and LinkedIn profile, not just a company name
- Multi-stakeholder visibility: see the buying committee forming in real-time
- Speed to action: reach out to the actual person, not guess who to contact

### The Analogy
"ABM is like sending a letter to a building. PBM is like sending a letter to a person. Same building. Wildly different conversion rate."

## Supporting Arguments

### The Math Angle
"You have 200 target accounts. Your ABM platform tells you 40 are showing intent. Great. That's 40 companies. Now what? You still need to find the right person at each one. With person-level ID, you don't start with 40 companies. You start with the 12 people at those companies who actually visited your site this week."

### The Outbound Saturation Angle
"Outbound is saturated. Everyone sends emails. The response rates are dropping. The teams that win are the ones reaching out to people who already showed interest. Person-level ID turns cold outbound into warm outbound."

### The Sales Diagnosis Angle
"A great doctor doesn't prescribe without diagnosing. A great sales team doesn't pitch without understanding the buyer's context. Person-level data gives your team the diagnostic information they need before the first call."

### The VC Learning Angle
"Go vertical. Win deeply. Don't try to be everything to everyone." This applies to the ABM evolution: stop targeting every account. Focus on the people who are already signaling interest.

## What Makes This Pillar Work

- Eli's credibility as a CEO thinking about industry evolution
- Balanced takes -- acknowledges what ABM got right before showing the limitation
- Revenue math that makes the gap concrete
- Extended analogies that make the concept intuitive
- Forward-looking vision that positions MidBound at the leading edge

## Safety

Medium risk. When discussing ABM platforms:
- Acknowledge their value. 6sense and Demandbase do real things for enterprise teams.
- The critique is the approach (account-level), not the product quality
- Never claim MidBound replaces 6sense or Demandbase (they do much more than ID)
- Position MidBound as the evolution, not the replacement
