# Content Pillar: Contrarian Takes

## Overview

Provocative, opinion-driven content that challenges industry conventions. Sebastian's primary pillar. The posts that start arguments, get shared, and position MidBound as the company willing to say what others won't.

## Primary Founder

Sebastian Obadia. This is his natural territory. Aggressive, direct, names names.

Eli can write contrarian takes too, but his version is more structured and invites debate ("Prove me wrong.") rather than declaring war.

## Format

### Sebastian Contrarian Take
1. **Bold opening claim** (1 line, lowercase): "company-level ID is a scam. Snitcher. Factors. All of them."
2. **The setup** (2-3 short paragraphs): Why the conventional wisdom is wrong. Specific examples.
3. **The evidence** (2-3 short paragraphs): MidBound's results, customer stories, or revenue math that proves the point.
4. **The challenge** (1-2 lines): Command or dare. "Stop guessing who visited. Start knowing."
5. **CTA** (1 line): Trial link or call to action.

### Eli Contrarian Take
1. **Reframe opening** (1-2 lines): "We don't have a lead gen problem. We have a visibility problem."
2. **The analogy or math** (3-4 paragraphs): Extended analogy or revenue math that builds the argument.
3. **The balanced take** (1-2 paragraphs): "Don't get me wrong though..." acknowledges the other side.
4. **The thesis** (1-2 lines): Clear conclusion that contradicts the conventional view.
5. **Sign-off**: "Prove me wrong."

## Contrarian Angles

### The Core Contrarian Claim
"Company-level identification is dead. The entire industry built around telling you which company visited your site is solving the wrong problem."

### Supporting Angles
- **"97% waste"**: Most companies are comfortable losing 97% of their website traffic. They shouldn't be.
- **"ABM is half-blind"**: Account-based marketing without person-level data is marketing to a building, not a person.
- **"Your CRM is lying"**: Most CRMs are full of enriched data that sales never uses because it's not connected to real-time behavior.
- **"Speed to lead is a myth without ID"**: You can't be fast if you don't know who to reach.
- **"Ad spend accountability"**: You can tie every dollar to a person, not just a session. Most companies don't because their tools can't.
- **"ICP is too tight"**: Companies box their ICP so tight they kill their own pipeline. Person-level data shows you who's actually interested, not just who fits the spreadsheet.

## What Makes This Pillar Work

- Specificity: names competitors, cites numbers, references real scenarios
- Conviction: picks a side and stands on it
- Backed by results: not just opinion, but MidBound's traction as evidence
- Memorable: the bold claims get shared even by people who disagree

## Safety

Medium risk. Naming competitors is intentional and allowed. But follow the safety filters:
- Critique the approach (company-level ID), not the companies' teams
- Never name competitor customers
- Never claim to know competitor internal metrics
- Acknowledge competitor strengths where relevant (especially in Eli's balanced version)
