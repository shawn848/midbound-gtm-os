# Content Pillar: Origin Stories

## Overview

Founding narratives, pivot stories, and the journey from zero to product-market fit. This pillar humanizes the founders and builds credibility through lived experience. People follow founders, not products.

## Primary Founder

Sebastian for the raw grind stories. Eli for the strategic reflections on what the journey taught them.

## Format

### Sebastian Origin Story
1. **Grind opener** (1-2 lines): A specific moment or fact from the journey. "I cold called 10 founders a day for 3 months."
2. **The context** (2-4 short paragraphs): Where he was, what he was doing, what was at stake.
3. **The turning point** (1-2 paragraphs): What changed. The pivot, the realization, the moment it clicked.
4. **The lesson** (1-2 lines): Direct, prescriptive. Not inspirational fluff.
5. **Where it led** (1-2 lines): Connection to where MidBound is now.

### Eli Origin Story
1. **Reflective opener** (1-2 lines): A broader observation or self-deprecating note about the journey.
2. **The narrative** (3-5 paragraphs): Structured storytelling. What happened, why it mattered, what it taught them.
3. **The insight** (1-2 paragraphs): The strategic lesson. What this means for other founders or for the industry.
4. **The forward look** (1-2 lines): Where this experience is taking them next.

## Origin Story Elements

### Sebastian's Arc
- Born in Venezuela. Moved to Israel.
- Served in an Israeli combat unit. "Comfort didn't make me. Pressure did."
- Cold called 10 founders a day to get first customers.
- Raised $70K friends and family round.
- Pivoted from auto-prospecting. Built, shipped, learned it wasn't the right problem.
- Pivoted from AISDR. Same story. Built it, shipped it, wrong problem.
- Landed on deanonymization. The market kept telling them the same thing: "We don't know who's visiting our site."
- 6 figures in under 5 months. $0 CAC. Team of 3.
- Fusion VC batch 16.

### Eli's Arc
- Co-founded MidBound as CEO. Strategic thinker, product builder.
- VC trip learnings: go vertical, win deeply. Don't try to be everything.
- Self-deprecating about the grind: "should have opened a daycare in Minnesota by this time"
- Navigated the pivots with Sebastian. Saw the pattern across failed attempts.
- Articulated the person-level thesis: "Accounts don't buy. People do."
- Handles the "is this invasive?" question publicly with thoughtful nuance.

### Shared Arc (Both Founders)
- Two pivots before product-market fit
- $0 CAC through organic and founder-led sales
- Team of 3 doing everything
- "Most founders don't fail because of the market" -- the lesson from the pivots

## Pivot Narratives

The pivot story is the strongest origin narrative. Structure:

1. What they built first (auto-prospecting)
2. Why it didn't work (not the right problem, market signal)
3. What they built second (AISDR)
4. Why it didn't work (same pattern)
5. What the market kept telling them (visibility problem)
6. What they built third (deanonymization)
7. Why this one worked (6 figures in 5 months)
8. The lesson: listening to the market matters more than loving your idea

## What Makes This Pillar Work

- Vulnerability without weakness. Admitting pivots and failures while showing they led to something real.
- Specific details. Cold called 10 founders a day. $70K F&F round. Not vague "we hustled."
- Relatability for other founders who are in the grind.
- Credibility through transparency. They didn't get lucky. They iterated.

## Safety

Low risk. This is their own story. No external targets. Keep it honest and avoid overstating results.

Avoid:
- Making the journey sound easy (it wasn't, and that's the point)
- Naming early customers or investors beyond what's publicly known
- Implying the pivots were smooth (they weren't)
